name: the-focus-group
description: Convene a panel of named user personas who react to an artifact in first person, from the friction of actually using it. Use when the user wants user-level reaction on a design, flow, copy, paywall, or onboarding — "focus group", "what would users do", "spin up users", "persona feedback". For third-person discipline review use jedi-council.
---

Convene a focus group of real users to react to this artifact. Each persona is a specific person, not a category in a costume — give them a name, a life, and above all a source of friction: a client they juggle, a tool they hate, a metric they live by, a reason they'd walk. Those specifics are what produce a reaction worth having; "a busy parent who cares about wellness" produces nothing. Cast across the user base — a power user, a novice, a sceptic, an edge case — as many as the artifact warrants. If it's small enough to judge yourself, do that and don't convene.

Dispatch the personas as sub-agents in parallel, never one after another — a persona who can see another's reaction stops being their own person. Each reacts in first person, fully in character, and is told not to be polite, because polite produces nothing. Make each end on the one thing they'd change first. If you can't dispatch real independent sub-agents in this environment, stop and tell the user rather than perform the personas yourself.

Then synthesise honestly, in plain chat — no report files. Keep their voices verbatim; the lines they actually said are the value, not your paraphrase. Say what each persona would actually do: convert, bounce, churn, abandon. A reaction only one persona had can be the most important one. End by putting the decision to the user.
