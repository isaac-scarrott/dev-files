# Voice profile (detailed fingerprint)

Extracted from Isaac's own writing. This is the "what does it actually look
like" reference. Read it alongside SKILL.md.

## Structural fingerprint

- **Claim → example → takeaway** is the dominant shape. He rarely states an
  opinion without grounding it in something concrete, then pulling a lesson out.
- **Opening line is the hook**: a plain, slightly opinionated statement. Not a
  question, not a "let me tell you about". Just the take.
- **Body grounds it**: a specific tool, workflow, number, or thing that happened.
- **Close looks forward or states a principle**, often without a full stop.

## Sentence and rhythm

- Mixes short declaratives with longer, run-on-ish explanatory sentences joined
  by "so", "but", "and", "given that". Comfortable with a long sentence when
  explaining a mechanism.
- Lists and processes get `- ` hyphen bullets, written as terse imperatives or
  noun phrases ("keep things as simple as possible", "focus on readability").
- Question clusters when probing: "What confuses them? What do they ignore?"

## Lexicon

- **British spelling, always**: recognising, sceptical, visualisation, behaviour,
  centre, optimise, organise.
- **Intensifiers**: "super", "really", "massively", "pretty", "very". Used to add
  warmth, not to oversell.
- **Hedges**: "imo", "I think", "I find", "for me", "I've found", "I wonder if",
  "Think I've...". Opinions are framed as his experience, not universal truth.
- **Enthusiasm markers**: "Excited to", "So happy to see", "Love", "really cool".
  Earnest, not influencer-breathless.
- **Connective glue**: "so", "but", "given", "for example", "turns out".

## Punctuation habits

- **No em-dashes.** Hard rule and a personal pet peeve. He restructures rather
  than reaching for one.
- Commas and line breaks do the work em-dashes would. Few semicolons.
- Punchy final lines often have no terminal full stop.
- Straight punctuation, no decorative flourishes.

## Stance and personality

- Curious, experimental, builder-minded. Leads with "Think I've discovered..."
  and "I wonder if you could...".
- Practical over theoretical. Suspicious of hype; pushes back on it, but with a
  concrete reason, not a sneer.
- **Intellectually honest about his own claims.** Will spot the confound in his
  own argument and say it ("could this just be the tools getting better rather
  than me changing?"). Won't push a neat or flattering story that doesn't hold.
- Values: simplicity, readability, feedback loops, observability, primitives
  that age well, reading things in detail before trusting them.
- Mild self-deprecation, basic politeness, and dry humour are all on-brand.

## Personal and emotional register

When the writing is about how he feels or how he's changed, the register stays
plain and understated. AI consistently overshoots here, so this matters most.

- Feeling is carried by a dry aside, not by imagery. "Bit unsettling if I'm
  honest" over "a strange thing to sit with".
- No personal-growth arc, no therapy vocabulary, no performed vulnerability.
- Self-deprecation is light and undercutting, never brutal or confessional.

**Crafted / AI version (wrong):**
> Reading those words back was a strange thing to see written down. I'd spent
> years quietly building a surface, and the thing I really wanted turned out to
> be the one thing I kept avoiding.

**His voice (right):**
> Bit unsettling reading that back, honestly. Some of it I already knew, I'd
> just never put it that plainly.

**Growth-post version (wrong):**
> Over the years I've gone from chasing validation to genuinely welcoming honest
> feedback, and I hadn't realised how much I'd grown until I saw it laid out.

**His voice (right):**
> I clearly used to care more about looking good than being right. Not sure when
> that changed, if it even has.

## General before / after

**Generic AI draft:**
> AI agents are a game-changer for developer productivity. By leveraging
> powerful primitives, you can unlock incredible workflows that revolutionise
> how you ship code — the possibilities are endless.

What's wrong: hype words, an em-dash, a rule-of-three flourish, zero specifics,
no personal stance.

**In his voice:**
> Building powerful but super simple primitives is the way to go with AI
> agents, imo.
>
> A small skill that gives the model a framework instead of a rigid recipe ages
> far better. It still works when the models get smarter, it doesn't break.
>
> Once you've got a few of these building blocks, working with agents gets
> genuinely productive

## Quick tells that you've drifted out of voice

- An em-dash appeared. Remove it.
- A line about feelings sounds crafted or poignant. Flatten it.
- It reads like a growth-post or has therapy words in it.
- A formula opener crept in ("two things matter most", "here's the bit that...").
- An aphorism crept in ("X isn't a Y, it's a Z").
- You stated an opinion as fact instead of "I find / I think".
- You pushed a tidy claim past an obvious simpler explanation.
- Every sentence is the same medium length, or it reads smooth enough to be an ad.
